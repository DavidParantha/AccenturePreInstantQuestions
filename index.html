1. Synonyms & Antonyms (Definition & Master Guide)

• Synonym: A word that has the SAME or nearly identical meaning as another word.
Example: Ephemeral = Fleeting / Transient (lasting a very short time), Lucid = Clear / Understandable.
• Antonym: A word that has the OPPOSITE meaning.
Example: Diligent (hardworking) ↔ Lazy / Indolent, Abundant (plentiful) ↔ Scarce / Sparse.

🎯 Golden Elimination Strategy in Accenture:

Look at the emotional tone (+ or -) of the word:

  • If the word is positive (e.g., Amiable = friendly), eliminate negative answer choices (hostile, rude).
  • Prefix Clues: mal- (bad: malicious, malfunction), bene- (good: beneficial, benevolent), in-/im- (opposite: impossible, invisible).

2. Prepositions & Fixed Exam Collocations

A Preposition connects a noun or pronoun to other words in the sentence to indicate time, location, direction, or relationship.

⚠️ Accenture's #1 Grammar Trap — Fixed Prepositions: Certain verbs and adjectives MUST ALWAYS take one specific preposition:

Rule / PhraseCorrect Usage ✅Common Trap (Wrong) ❌
Prefer ... toI prefer coffee to tea.I prefer coffee than tea.
Accused ofHe was accused of theft.He was accused for theft.
Congratulate onCongratulate her on winning.Congratulate her for winning.
Good at / Bad atShe is very good at mathematics.She is very good in mathematics.
Prevent / Abstain fromPrevented him from leaving.Prevented him to leave.
Different fromThis model is different from that.This model is different than that.
Senior / Superior toHe is senior to me.He is senior than me.
Look forward to + V-ingI look forward to meeting you.I look forward to meet you.

3. Articles (A, An, The) — The Sound System

Articles indicate whether a noun is general/unspecific (A, An) or specific/unique (The).

🔥 The Golden Law: The choice between A and An depends on the INITIAL PHONETIC SOUND, not the English spelling!

ArticleRule & ReasonReal Exam Examples
Use "A"Before words starting with a Consonant Sound (even if written with vowel letter 'U' or 'E') a university (sounds like 'yu-ni-ver-sity')
a European country (sounds like 'yu-ro-pe-an')
a one-rupee coin (sounds like 'wun')
Use "An"Before words starting with a Vowel Sound (even if written with consonant letter 'H' or 'M') an MBA graduate (pronounced 'em-bee-ay')
an honest officer (silent 'H', sounds like 'on-est')
an hour (silent 'H', sounds like 'our')
an MP / an SP (pronounced 'em-pee', 'es-pee')
Use "The"Specific items, unique entities, rivers, oceans, superlatives the Nile River, the Pacific Ocean, the tallest building

4. Subject-Verb Agreement & Tense Sequences

  • Parenthetical Connectors ("Along with / As well as / Together with"): The verb agrees strictly with the FIRST subject.
    The manager, along with his developers, WAS present. (NOT 'were')
  • Neither...Nor / Either...Or: The verb agrees with the NEAREST subject.
    Neither Rohit nor his teammates HAVE arrived. ('teammates' is plural -> 'have')
  • Past Perfect Sequencing (Two Past Events): The EARLIER past event takes had + V3, and the later past action takes Simple Past (V2).
    The train HAD LEFT before we REACHED the platform.

1. Directional Sense & Pythagoras Shortcuts

Direction problems involve plotting movement along 4 cardinal directions (North, South, East, West).

📐 Pythagoras Shortest Distance Formula:

Shortest Distance = √(Δx² + Δy²)

Triples to Memorize for 5-Second Answers:
• (3, 4, 5) $\rightarrow$ 3 km North + 4 km East = 5 km distance.
• (6, 8, 10) $\rightarrow$ 6 km North + 8 km East = 10 km distance.
• (5, 12, 13) $\rightarrow$ 5 km West + 12 km North = 13 km distance.

☀️ Shadow Direction Rules:

  • Morning (Sunrise): Sun is in the EAST $\rightarrow$ Shadows fall towards the WEST.
  • Evening (Sunset): Sun is in the WEST $\rightarrow$ Shadows fall towards the EAST.
  • 12:00 Noon: Sun is directly overhead $\rightarrow$ No shadow.

2. Core CS (OS, DBMS, Networking)

• Operating Systems: 4 Deadlock Conditions: Mutual Exclusion, Hold and Wait, No Preemption, Circular Wait.
• DBMS & SQL: TRUNCATE is DDL (instant, no rollback); DELETE is DML (row-by-row, logged).
• Networking: Layer 3 (Network) routes IP packets. TCP is connection-oriented & reliable (3-way handshake); UDP is connectionless & fast (streaming).
